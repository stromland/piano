# A key is not a scale

The app separates **Scale** (a set of notes) from **Key** (a tonic plus a
tonality, and the chords composers actually use in it). Deriving a key's
chords from its scale alone would teach the wrong thing in minor: the natural
minor scale of A gives Em as the fifth chord, but music in A minor nearly always
uses E or E7, borrowed from harmonic minor, because its leading tone pulls home.
So a minor key shows its natural chords plus V and vii° marked as borrowed, and
the scale list stays a list of note sets that knows nothing about harmony.

## Considered options

- **Key = scale seen as chords.** Simple and uniform, but shows v as the
  dominant of every minor key.
- **Harmonic minor as the minor key's scale.** Gets V right, but gives an
  augmented III+ that minor-key music rarely uses and hides the natural v, VII.
